Behind the Build

My notes on developing and testing PawPilot.

My original idea

Before I used AI, my hunch was that a small doggy daycare business would pay for something that helped them keep track of bookings and vaccinations, and give owners updates about their dogs. I wanted it to save staff time.

Who I was aiming it at

I was focusing on small, independent doggy daycares in Ireland, rather than trying to make something for every type of pet business.

Working with AI on the design

The first layout AI gave me was bland and not completely user-friendly. It didn't fully follow my prompt either. I went back and forth with AI a lot to change the design. I also researched how to make things simpler and more like one-tap actions, and changed the colours and layout to make it look sleeker.

Images I tried

I selected some images from Pixabay, but then realised the versions I'd found had marks on them and weren't suitable to use as they were. I ended up moving away from that idea.

A problem with the landing page

When I tried the demo link on my PawPilot landing page, I kept getting a 404 error. I spent about 20 minutes trying to work out why, before noticing that the website address in the code was missing a hyphen between Isabel and Nestor.

Testing the demo and making changes

The demo had a quick-add option for dogs already booked into the system, but there was no way to add a completely new dog. I spotted that during testing and went back to add the option. I also tested checking in a dog whose vaccinations weren't up to date, and the system wouldn't let me check the dog in.

Pupdates and saving time

I tested the Pupdate feature and it worked as I expected in the demo. Staff could choose quick, one-tap updates such as 'Nap time' or 'Out for a walk', or type their own reply. My idea was for SMS, WhatsApp and Instagram to be accessible from one staff portal, rather than staff having to switch between different apps. I haven't confirmed that the demo actually sends messages through those services.
